Geometric Algebra

Unifying the abstract logic of symbols with the physical reality of space.

The Rationale

Algebra is often taught as a set of rules for moving letters around an equals sign. In this hub, we view algebra through the lens of Geometric Geometry. Every equation is a constraint—a rule that defines a path in space.

Solving simultaneous equations is not "calculating a variable"; it is the physical act of finding an intersection. When two constraints are met at once, they define a unique location: a coordinate pair \((x, y)\).

From \(y=mx+c\) to \(ax+by=c\)

While \(y=mx+c\) describes a function (input \(x\), output \(y\)), the Standard Form \(ax+by=c\) describes a relationship. It is far more powerful for understanding the boundaries of our coordinate system:

The y-axis is simply the line where \(x = 0\)
The x-axis is simply the line where \(y = 0\)

By setting variables to zero, we find where paths intersect the "edges" of our world:

  • Roots: The intersection of your line with the constraint \(y=0\).
  • Intercepts: The intersection of your line with the constraint \(x=0\).
